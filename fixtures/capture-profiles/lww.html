Contraception in ADPKD

Contraception is important in patients with ADPKD and polycystic liver disease (PLD).

Nonhormonal contraception methods include barrier methods and copper intrauterine devices.

Imaging (magnetic resonance imaging/computed tomography), with liver volumetry, can support shared decision-making when selecting the most appropriate contraceptive method.

Pregnancy in ADPKD

ADPKD affects approximately one in 1000 and is the most common monogenic kidney disease.

Pregnancy in ADPKD is associated with higher rates of gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, preterm birth, and intrauterine growth restriction.

Comprehensive Monitoring during Pregnancy

  • Urinary tract infections : Pregnant patients with ADPKD have a 14% higher risk to develop urinary tract infections.
  • Urinary obstruction : During pregnancy, there is a risk of physiological hydronephrosis.
  • Proteinuria and kidney function : ADPKD is typically nonproteinuric, any proteinuria warrants close monitoring.
  • Hypertension : It is common in ADPKD, often arises early and is linked to kidney enlargement.
  • Preeclampsia : Women with ADPKD are at increased risk of preeclampsia and preterm delivery.
  • Fetal evaluation : Enlarged or echogenic kidneys on ultrasound may suggest ADPKD, especially with a parental history.
